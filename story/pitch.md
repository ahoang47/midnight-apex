# Street Racing RPG — Pitch Document

## High Concept

A narrative-driven street racing RPG set in a moody, night-only fictional city (name `<TBD>`; a mashup that is neither Japan nor the US) where players progress through interconnected underground racing scenes using a tactical, decision-based racing system.

The player does not directly control the vehicle. Instead, races are resolved through strategic inputs, timing-based reactions, opponent prediction, and risk management systems that transform street racing into high-speed psychological combat.

Inspired by:

- Initial D
- Tokyo Drift
- Racing Lagoon
- JoJo’s Bizarre Adventure
- Pokémon-style progression structures

---

# Core Gameplay Hook

## Tactical Racing Combat (No Driving Control)

Races are structured as tactical encounters rather than traditional racing gameplay.

Players dynamically choose a stat for each phase of a corner (Entry, Apex, Exit):

- POW
- EN
- HAN

On screen each choice shows as a lane on the road (OUT for POW, MID for HAN, IN for EN).

These decisions interact with:

- opponent behavior patterns
- momentum states
- pressure systems
- EN (endurance)
- vehicle build characteristics

Critical race moments trigger cinematic QTE sequences:

- overtakes
- defensive holds
- drafting clashes
- corner pressure battles
- near-collision recoveries

The goal is to create:

- readable strategy
- emotional tension
- expressive racing styles

without relying on simulator driving execution.

Races move fast, so Jesse's DAQ keeps the last race for playback: the DAQ log lists every corner with the player's line and the opponent's line, so the player can study what happened (canon 29).

# Core Gameplay Loop

1. Explore the city at night
2. Discover racers, crews, rumors, and race organizers
3. Participate in tactical race encounters
4. Earn money, parts, and reputation
5. Upgrade and tune vehicle builds
6. Increase standing across multiple racing scenes
7. Unlock higher-tier race invitations and the next district's crew
8. Discover Myth Drivers and hidden story events
9. Revisit districts as reputation opens new opportunities

---

# Progression Structure

Prologue → Rounds 1–3 → Finale. Each district has one crew and crew boss. Rounds play in order, one district each: Round 1 Street, Round 2 Wangan, Round 3 Touge. Each round the player runs that district's whole crew once (its kids, mature members, old heads, then the boss), with no rematches, and meets that district's Myth Driver afterward. Finishing Round 3 unlocks the Myth Drivers and the summit. See `canon.md` and `plot.md`.

# Boss Structure

Crew bosses are not simple end-of-level encounters.

They are:

- recurring presences within the world
- ideological embodiments of their racing discipline
- highly stylized personality figures inspired by JoJo-style presentation

Players may:

- hear about bosses long before racing them
- encounter them indirectly
- see them influence district culture before official challenges occur

Boss challenges require:

- sufficient reputation
- crew access
- organizer approval
- demonstrated tactical competency

# Camera Direction

## Overworld Exploration

Inspired by Racing Lagoon and PS1-era Final Fantasy environments:

- angled semi-isometric perspective
- atmospheric nighttime navigation
- stylized urban exploration

## Racing View

- third-person cinematic race framing, fixed behind the player
- the rival's lane reads as Entry when that rival is ahead; the drawn lead is capped so it stays readable, and that spacing is not the real gap
- when the player is ahead, the trailer falls back into the top-center mirror and is harder to read on purpose

The spacing and Entry rule is `design/06-pattern-read.md` (Lane Entry and visual gap). Do not add a marker, enlarge the lamps, or cap the trailer to make that mirror as easy to read as the view ahead.

## QTE Moments

Camera becomes highly cinematic:

- dramatic perspective shifts
- aggressive motion framing
- close-up pressure shots

Inspired by:

- Naughty Dog cinematic presentation philosophy

# Narrative Overview

## Premise

The player is a young adult (any gender) relocating to a new city for work.

The opening establishes:

- arrival at dusk (the game is set at night from then on)
- nighttime isolation
- underground car enthusiasm

The player is pulled into the racing scene after a scripted race encounter ends in catastrophic engine failure.

The opposing racer — later revealed as the mentor — helps rebuild the destroyed engine and introduces the player to the city’s underground racing ecosystem.

---

# Mentor Character

The mentor is Sabine:

- always female (the player character can be any gender)
- approximately 10+ years older
- a respected mid-tier elite racer
- one of the only known drivers capable of competing across all disciplines at a high level

The mentor is grounded and restrained compared to the city’s more stylized personalities.

They serve as:

- guide figure
- emotional anchor
- gateway into underground racing culture

---

# Myth Drivers

Three legendary former racers exist within the city’s history:

- Touge specialist (Myth Driver #1)
- Wangan specialist (Myth Driver #2)
- Street specialist (Myth Driver #3) — in this universe, circuit and street racing are the same scene; he wanted out to go pro, but the sweep cost him the offer (impounded and charged that night)

These racers:

- are no longer active rulers of the scene
- are considered washed-up or semi-retired legends
- still command enormous respect from current crew bosses
- are no longer friends with each other, and have not spoken since the breakup
- get mythologized by the scene similarly to how SpaceX employees are in the books by Eric Berger

# Core Story Mystery

A major police sweep years earlier fractured the old underground racing generation.

The sweep itself was benign and coincidental — there was no informant. The snitch story was a convenient cover each Myth Driver used, privately, for his own personal reason to push the mentor out.

The accusation never spread. The old generation froze the mentor out quietly; the wider scene only knows she walked away without a word around the time of the sweep. She's still around, but she keeps clear of every crew.

The truth is never presented directly.

Players gradually uncover fragmented and contradictory perspectives through:

- crew member dialogue
- crew boss exposition
- Myth Driver encounters
- mentor interactions
- environmental storytelling

The mystery evolves from:

“What happened?”

into:

“Can anyone involved actually see this objectively anymore?”

The player constantly pressures the mentor for answers throughout the game, but the mentor repeatedly deflects or avoids discussing the past. She genuinely believes she was frozen out over the snitch accusation and does not know the real reasons.

Nobody tells the player about the accusation; they piece it together from clues in the crew reveals — and the clues are bad data that point toward the mentor’s guilt. At the end of Round 3, the player accuses the mentor of being the snitch. She doesn’t defend herself; she’s been accused once already and won’t waste the energy again. After a long silence: “Get out of my garage.” and the relationship blows up going into the Finale.

Blaming the Myth Driver who challenged them on the mountain for wrecking everything, the player goes back up and races him, and that race sets off the races with the other two. The player races out of blame, not to find the truth, but the truth comes out by accident: their accounts contradict each other, and the snitch story unravels: the police sweep was benign; there was no informant, only each Myth Driver’s personal reason. The player goes to the mentor: “I know what really happened.” She blows up at them for digging into her past, a second wound after the accusation, and the player leaves. Then the player convinces all three Myth Drivers to come and apologize too: one big apology party. It is the first time they have met since the breakup.

- the mentor learns the real reasons for the first time, from the Myth Drivers
- the player owns that they made the same mistake the Myth Drivers did, and apologizes
- the Myth Drivers apologize too
- reconciliation occurs
- the former generation offers the mentor a place beside them again

After the player’s apology, the mentor calmly declines, declaring she already found a new crew:
the one formed around the player during the game. She also won’t go back to a group the Street specialist let her be pushed out of, rather than face telling her he was leaving.

She then gets in one angry parting shot: "My uncle's doing fine, by the way. Since none of you ever asked." Then she turns it back on the player: "And when are you calling them?"

In the epilogue, the player finally calls the hometown crew they walked out on, with a message: "I was wrong. Be there." They come to Fushigi-Yama, and after an awkward apology they settle it the only way they know: a low-stakes race, just for fun.
