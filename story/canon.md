## Decisions

1. **Three Myth Drivers, one per district.** #1 Touge, #2 Wangan, #3 Street. There is no #4.
2. **No informant.** The police sweep was benign and coincidental. The snitch accusation was a convenient story each Myth Driver used, privately, to cover his own personal reason for pushing Sabine out:
   - #1 — distrust of her uncle-dad, a detective (all three Myth Drivers knew about her uncle; it was no secret, but only #1 held it against her)
   - #2 — she turned down his date request
   - #3 — he wanted out to go pro and didn't want her hurt by him leaving the group, so he was glad she was gone; letting her be pushed out was easier on his feelings and harder on hers (see 9)
   - Always called the **police sweep** (not "sting operation").
   - **The accusation never spread.** Only the three Myth Drivers and Sabine know about it, and that she was pushed out. They froze her out quietly. The wider scene only knows she walked away from the old generation without a word around the time of the sweep and hasn't run with any crew since. She's still around (she runs her own garage and still drives), she just doesn't mess with the crews; crews speculate and misremember, but nobody names a snitch accusation.
3. **Sabine doesn't know.** She genuinely believes she was frozen out over the snitch accusation and learns the real reasons only at the end. She has never told anyone about it.
4. **Climax at the summit.** There is no trick. The player honestly invites Sabine to the summit of **Fushigi-Yama**, the Touge district's mountain, to apologize ("I was wrong. Be there." — first-pass line), the first time the PC breaks their pattern of leaving without explanation. The three Myth Drivers each, separately, catch wind that the PC is apologizing to Sabine, feel guilty, and show up on their own, perhaps also wanting to apologize `<TODO: how word reaches the Myth Drivers>`. They don't coordinate; the summit is still their first meeting since the breakup, and it's where the truth comes out. Racing each Myth Driver in his district during the Finale stays.
5. **Ending.** Sabine calmly declines their offer to rejoin, for three real reasons (the third: nobody ever acknowledged the family emergency she left for the night of the sweep, decision 23), the first two being: she has the player's crew now (which requires the PC's apology and repair after the rift — see 15), and #3 let her be pushed out to spare himself a goodbye (see 9), then gets in one cutting parting shot: "My uncle's doing fine, by the way. Since none of you ever asked."
6. **Setting.** A fictional mashup city, neither Japan nor the US. No real-world places or currencies. City name, in-world district names and currency are `<TBD>`.
7. **Time of day.** The opening happens at dusk; the game is at night from then on.
8. **Gender.** Sabine is always female. The player character can be any gender.
9. **Myth Driver #3 is the Street specialist.** Circuit and street racing are the same idea in this universe. He wanted out of the group to go pro in the circuit. He was glad Sabine was gone, not because he didn't want her around, but because he didn't want her hurt by him leaving. Letting her be pushed out was easier on his feelings and harder on hers. He never actually went pro: he let her be pushed out for a career that never happened `<TBD: why his pro career fell through>`. This is also a real reason Sabine won't rejoin him and the old generation at the end. He mirrors the PC: leaving without facing the goodbye.
10. **Silence since the breakup.** The Myth Drivers haven't spoken since the breakup. Anything about them talking to each other happened before it. The summit is their first meeting since.
11. **Act structure.** Prologue → Rounds 1–3 → Finale.
    - **Prologue:** dusk arrival, the overworld tutorial on the drive home from work, the race with Sabine, the blowout, the garage intro and the debt.
    - **Rounds 1–3:** each district has exactly one crew and one crew boss (three crews, three crew bosses in total). Each round the player faces each district’s crew and its crew boss in Touge, then Wangan, then Street, in that order; Rounds 2 and 3 are rematches against the same crew, which gets stronger every time. Every round, the player runs through the whole crew in rank order: first its kids who inherited the myth (they believe the legend as told), then its more mature members who know not everything is what it seems, then its old heads who were there, and finally the crew boss. When the player comes back the next round, the boss has leveled up the whole crew. Flavor text after each race reveals a little more. That progression matches the contradicting clues the PC uncovers across the game. Every crew defeat reveals another piece of the police sweep mystery.
    - **Round themes:** Round 1 = the incident, Round 2 = its consequences across the scene, Round 3 = distortion (what people misremember), each seen from every district.
    - **Myth Driver sightings:** each Myth Driver makes a first sighting right after his district's Round 1 crew is cleared: #1 after Round 1 Touge, #2 after Round 1 Wangan, #3 after Round 1 Street. At each sighting the Myth Driver talks with the PC; there's no race yet. The conversations build the mythos around the fallout and drive the PC to dig further, but never reveal the private accusation. The races stay in the Finale, in the same order: Touge (#1) → Wangan (#2) → Street (#3).
    - **Finale:** finishing Round 3 unlocks the three Myth Driver encounters (one per district), then the Fushigi-Yama summit and the ending.
    - The main plot stays grounded; JRPG weirdness and extra lore go in substories and side quests.
12. **Street cred gates the rotation.** After the player beats a crew, it won't give them a rematch until they've proven themselves in the other districts: they don't have enough street cred yet. Cred is earned by beating the other districts' crews, which is why each round rotates Touge → Wangan → Street. Street cred is an in-world lore explanation only, not a tracked stat.
13. **Player character (first pass).** The PC is mid-20s and any gender, moved to the city for work, but is really running from something: they walked out on their hometown crew without explaining why. The PC was the crew's driver and left the week of a big race for a job in the city without telling anyone, so the crew believes the PC ditched them for something better. The real reason (locked 2026-11-02): the PC felt like the weak link, believed they were holding the crew back and that it would do better without them, and left rather than say so. The crew never saw it that way. It's a misread, like the Myth Drivers', and the epilogue race shows the crew wanted the PC all along. This mirrors Sabine, who was pushed out of her crew over a lie.
    - **The PC's hook:** in Round 1 the PC hears Sabine's story (someone who walked away from her crew without explanation, whom some people assume had something to hide) and recognizes their own. Nobody calls Sabine a snitch there; the parallel is implied.
    - Helping Sabine reconcile is the PC working through their own guilt by proxy. After her parting shot at the summit, Sabine turns it back on them: "And when are you calling them?" After the rift (15) the mirror is literal: the PC accused Sabine on bad data, just as the Myth Drivers did.
    - **Epilogue (locked 2026-11-02):** the PC makes the call themselves, sending the hometown crew the same honest message they sent Sabine ("I was wrong. Be there."). Nobody else brings the crew (no clips or luck), or the PC's arc wouldn't resolve. The crew comes to Fushigi-Yama. After a short, awkward apology the crew doesn't fully forgive yet, they break the tension with a race ("Talk's cheap. Race us." — first pass). It's a low-stakes, just-for-fun downhill run that doesn't change the ending whether the PC wins or loses, possibly with Sabine, Jesse and Reiko watching. The race tuning belongs to Barry.
14. **Why Sabine takes the PC in.** Practical on the surface: her garage hasn't had a real driver since the breakup, and crews won't take her seriously without one. Personal underneath: she sees herself in the PC, someone who showed up alone and won't say why. She never admits the second.
15. **The PC puts it together — wrongly.** Nobody tells the PC about the snitch accusation. They work it out themselves from clues scattered across the crew reveals in Rounds 1–3, and the clues are bad data: they genuinely point toward Sabine's guilt. At the Mentor Confrontation (locked: end of Round 3) the PC accuses Sabine of being the snitch. She is hurt and refuses to defend herself, or denies it `<TODO: Sabine's reaction>`. It blows up their relationship, and the rift stands going into the Finale. Only at the summit does it come out that the police sweep was benign — the PC made the same mistake the Myth Drivers did.
16. **The PC's motive after the rift.** Two things drive the PC to chase the Myth Drivers and set up the summit: doubt (the Myth Drivers' accounts contradict each other, and the PC starts to suspect they were wrong about Sabine) and guilt (the rift echoes what the PC did back home, and they won't let it end the same way). Doubt drives the racing; guilt makes it personal.
17. **Myth Driver #2's guilt leaks.** In his Finale race, #2 hints that Sabine may have been a scapegoat, because he feels guilty about ousting her. It's one of the first cracks in the PC's certainty (feeds decision 16's doubt) and foreshadows him coming to the summit to apologize.
18. **Myth Driver #1's contradiction (first pass).** In his Finale race (the first of the three), #1 insists Sabine left on her own the night of the sweep. In the next race, #2's guilty hints say she was pushed. That's where the contradiction surfaces: the first time two Myth Driver accounts can't both be true, and it starts the PC's doubt (16).
19. **The debt.** Sabine fronted the engine rebuild, so the PC drives for her garage until it's paid off: a share of race winnings goes to the garage, and every crew the PC beats gives her garage the standing it hasn't had since the breakup (ties to decision 14). The debt is settled during Round 2 (exact point `<TBD>`). After that the PC stays because they're having fun and want to uncover the mystery behind Sabine. Until the debt is cleared, a share of each win goes to repaying it (not all of it), so money for mods is tight in Round 1, and the rest is what the PC spends on parts once wholesale opens. The share size is Barry's `<TBD>`. After Round 1, Reiko warms up to the PC and gives them wholesale prices on parts, even though the debt isn't cleared until Round 2, so by Round 3 the PC has plenty of access to parts (paying off her "Wholesale is for crew. You're not crew." line from the garage intro). The amount owed is a systems number, handed to Barry via Desmond `<TBD: debt amount, and the size of Reiko's wholesale discount — Barry>`.

20. **Not everyone holds it against her.** Most of the scene isn't bitter toward Sabine; they respect her and are puzzled by why she walked away. Suspicion comes from a few voices (gossiping kids, people repeating rumors), not from everyone.

21. **The Wangan crew boss is a promoter.** He organizes the highway runs, takes bets and gets a cut, and sees the PC as a headliner he can sell. He's pragmatic about Sabine, not bitter: her walking away cost the scene its best draw. After Round 2 he opens a high-stakes betting side game for extra money (mechanics are Barry's `<TBD>`). **Old flame (private):** he and Sabine had a thing back in the old generation's day, which is the real reason she turned down Myth Driver #2's date. It hits hard because #2 is the Wangan Myth Driver: the man she chose and the man she turned down share the same highway, and #2 has watched the promoter run his old turf for years without knowing. Neither the promoter nor Sabine talks about it; the PC only picks up hints (the way he talks about her), and the full truth comes out at the summit, when #2 finally learns why she said no (first pass).
22. **The Street crew boss is an influencer.** Young-ish and cocksure, he built his following on the street scene after the old generation broke up and isn't really about the old scene: Sabine and the Myth Drivers are relics to him. He reflects a lot of modern car culture (clout, builds made for the camera), in sharp contrast to the old generation. First pass: he idolized #3 growing up and felt abandoned when #3 left to "go pro".
23. **The sweep and the call.** The sweep was city-wide across all three districts and benign (routine enforcement). Sabine left minutes before because her uncle called about an unrelated family emergency: he'd had a heart attack. He survived but needed surgery and a couple of weeks of recovery, and Sabine stayed with him at the hospital and then at home, out of contact for about two weeks. That silence is exactly when the Myth Drivers decided she'd run. When she came back she was already frozen out, and nobody asked where she'd been. It stings more because the man she was caring for is the "cop uncle" #1 distrusted. While she was dealing with it, her friends accused her instead of showing up for her, and nobody has ever acknowledged what she went through. That's a third reason she won't rejoin at the end (alongside the PC's crew and #3, decision 5).

## Racer Types and Archetypes

"Racer type" is the story role (Delinquent, Salaryman, ...). "Archetype" is the driving style from the sim (`sim/velocity_shift/`). Each district has exactly one racer type per archetype; each racer type always uses its archetype. Only named rivals may break the pattern, on purpose.

| Archetype | Touge | Wangan | Street |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aggressive**: attacks right away | Delinquent | Executive | Punk |
| **Defensive**: waits, then punishes your habits | Gang Leader | Office Lady | Mechanic |
| **Precise**: always makes the smartest move | Drifter | Doctor | Engineer |
| **Balanced**: follows a set rhythm | High Schooler | Salaryman | Skater |
| **Adaptive**: cautious early, sharper later | Honor Student | Consultant | Influencer |

Never reuse archetype names as character or racer-type names. Crew boss stakes are money, rep, rare shop mods and story progress; losing a crew boss race means a retry, never losing the car.

## Backstory Timeline

1. **The old generation.** Sabine races alongside the three Myth Drivers. Pressure builds privately: everyone in the group knows her uncle-dad is a detective, and #1 never trusts her because of it; #2 resents the turned-down date (it stayed private; he wrongly believes she told people) `<CHECK: rewritten for private-rumor canon — Austin to confirm>`, #3 wants out to go pro and dreads telling her he's leaving.
2. **The police sweep.** A large, coordinated sweep hits all three districts (Touge, Wangan, Street) in one night: lots of tickets and impounds, maybe a few arrests, but routine enforcement, not a tip-off. Minutes before it starts, Sabine gets a call from her uncle-dad about an unrelated family emergency (he's had a heart attack) and leaves; she's the only one of the old generation who walks away clean. The scene goes quiet for months afterward (the "collapse"). Privately, the three Myth Drivers accuse Sabine of being the snitch, each using it as cover for his own reason, and freeze her out quietly. The accusation never leaves the four of them.
3. **The breakup.** The old generation fractures. To the wider scene, Sabine simply walked away from the old generation without a word around the time of the sweep. She stays on the scene but keeps clear of every crew. The Myth Drivers stop speaking to each other and fade into semi-retired legend. #3's pro career never happens `<TBD: why his pro career fell through>`. Sabine builds her own garage crew, still believing the accusation was the reason.
4. **The player arrives.** The player moves to the city at dusk. A first race with Sabine ends in a blown engine; she tows the car to her garage and the player enters the scene owing a repair debt. She needs a real driver; she also sees herself in the PC (unspoken).
5. **Rounds 1–3.** Each round the player beats the same three crews and their crew bosses (running through each whole crew from its kids to its mature members to its old heads to the boss, and each crew leveled up since last time) in Touge, then Wangan, then Street, earning the street cred for the next rematch; every crew defeat reveals another piece of the mystery of her disappearance, and in Round 1 the PC recognizes their own story in hers. In Round 1 each Myth Driver also makes a first sighting after his district's crew is cleared (no race yet). Sabine deflects. The PC pieces together the accusation from misleading clues in the crew reveals and, at the end of Round 3, accuses Sabine of being the snitch. The relationship blows up `<TODO: Sabine's reaction>`.
6. **The Myth Drivers.** Finishing Round 3 unlocks a race with each Myth Driver: Touge (#1), Wangan (#2), Street (#3). The player races them while estranged from Sabine and gathers contradictory accounts.
7. **The summit.** The player honestly asks Sabine to meet at the summit of Fushigi-Yama to apologize ("I was wrong. Be there."). The three Myth Drivers each hear about it separately, feel guilty, and come on their own. The truth comes out: the sweep was benign. Sabine learns the real reasons for the first time, and the PC realizes they were wrong too and has to own it.
8. **Ending.** They reconcile and offer Sabine her place back. After the PC's apology, she calmly declines (she has the player's crew, and she won't go back to the group #3 let her be pushed out of), and gets in one parting shot. Then she asks the PC, "And when are you calling them?"
9. **Epilogue.** The PC calls their hometown crew ("I was wrong. Be there."). The crew comes to Fushigi-Yama, the PC apologizes, and they run a low-stakes race for fun. The PC ends the game part of the scene with both crews.
