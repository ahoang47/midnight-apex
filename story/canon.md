## Decisions

1. **Three Myth Drivers, one per district.** #1 Touge, #2 Wangan, #3 Street. There is no #4.
   - **Mythologizing tone (2026-11-06):** the scene mythologizes the Myth Drivers similarly to how SpaceX employees get mythologized in the books by Eric Berger.

2. **No informant.** The police sweep was benign and coincidental (23). The snitch accusation was a convenient story each Myth Driver used, privately, to cover his own reason for pushing Sabine out:
   - #1: distrust of her uncle-dad, a detective. All three knew about the uncle (no secret), but only #1 held it against her.
   - #2: she turned down his date (the real reason she said no: 21).
   - #3: he wanted out to go pro; see 9.
   - Always called the **police sweep** (not "sting operation").
   - **The accusation never spread.** Only the three Myth Drivers and Sabine know about it, and that she was pushed out; they froze her out quietly. The wider scene only knows she walked away from the old generation without a word around the time of the sweep and hasn't run with any crew since. She's still around (runs her own garage, still drives) but keeps clear of the crews. Crews speculate and misremember, but nobody names a snitch accusation.

3. **Sabine doesn't know.** She believes she was frozen out over the snitch accusation and learns the real reasons only at the end. She has never told anyone about it.

4. **Climax at the summit.**
   - No trick. After the Finale races the PC knows what really happened (16) and goes to Sabine: "I know what really happened." She blows up at the PC for digging into her past, a second wound after the accusation (15), and the PC leaves.
   - The PC convinces all three Myth Drivers to come and apologize to her too: one big apology party.
   - The climax is a final race between the PC and Sabine at the summit of **Fushigi-Yama**, the Touge mountain. First pass for how the PC gets her there (2026-11-05, needs more passes; Open Question 8): once Reiko sees the PC rounding up the Myth Drivers to fix what they broke, she points the PC to where Sabine runs the mountain alone.
   - The PC's and the Myth Drivers' apologies happen around the race `<TODO: before, during or after the race>`.
   - The summit is the Myth Drivers' first meeting since the breakup (10), and where Sabine learns their real reasons. Racing each Myth Driver in his district during the Finale stays.

5. **Ending.** After the final race and the apology party, Sabine calmly declines the offer to rejoin, for three reasons:
   - she has the PC's crew now (requires the PC's apology and repair after the rift, 15);
   - #3 let her be pushed out to spare himself a goodbye (9);
   - nobody ever acknowledged the family emergency she left for the night of the sweep (23).
   - Then one angry parting shot, a rare moment of anger breaking through her guard: "My uncle's doing fine, by the way. Since none of you ever asked." Then she turns it on the PC (13).

6. **Setting.** A fictional mashup city, neither Japan nor the US. No real-world places or currencies. City name, in-world district names and currency are `<TBD>`.

7. **Time of day.** The opening happens at dusk; the game is at night from then on.

8. **Gender.** Sabine is always female. The player character can be any gender.

9. **Myth Driver #3 is the Street specialist.**
   - Circuit and street racing are the same scene in this universe. He wanted out of the group to go pro in the circuit.
   - He was glad Sabine was gone, not because he didn't want her around, but because he didn't want her hurt by him leaving. Letting her be pushed out was easier on his feelings and harder on hers.
   - He never went pro: the sweep cost him: he was impounded and charged that night, and the team courting him wouldn't touch a street racer with a record, so the offer died (locked 2026-11-04). It gives him a second reason to push Sabine away, on top of avoiding the goodbye: she walked away clean while he lost everything.
   - This is one of Sabine's reasons not to rejoin (5). He mirrors the PC: leaving without facing the goodbye.

10. **Silence since the breakup.** The Myth Drivers haven't spoken since the breakup; anything about them talking to each other happened before it. The summit is their first meeting since.

11. **Act structure: Prologue → Rounds 1–3 → Finale.**
    - **Prologue:** dusk arrival, the overworld tutorial on the drive home from work, the race with Sabine, the blowout, the garage intro and the debt.
    - **Rounds 1–3:** one crew and one crew boss per district (three of each). Rounds play in order (1 → 2 → 3); within each round the PC freely chooses which crew to challenge, and once they start a crew's ladder they're locked in until they beat its boss (12). Rounds 2 and 3 are rematches against the same crews.
    - **Crew order each round:** the kids who inherited the myth (believe the legend as told) → the more mature members who know not everything is what it seems → the old heads who were there → the crew boss. Next round the boss has leveled up the whole crew.
    - **Reveals:** flavor text after each race reveals a little more; every crew defeat reveals another piece of the police sweep mystery, matching the contradicting clues the PC uncovers (15).
    - **Round themes:** Round 1 = the incident, Round 2 = its consequences across the scene, Round 3 = distortion (what people misremember), each seen from every district.
    - **Myth Driver sightings:** a sighting after every round: each Myth Driver appears right after his district's crew is cleared in Rounds 1, 2 and 3 (#1 after Touge, #2 after Wangan, #3 after Street; nine in total). The Round 1 one is his first sighting. He talks with the PC; no race until the Finale. Each sighting fits its round theme (1 incident, 2 consequences, 3 distortion). Sightings only establish that each Myth Driver knew Sabine and push the PC to dig; they never reveal the private accusation and never lay out the sweep. Sweep information comes from crew reveals and post-race crew dialogue. Beats are in `plot.md` §4–6; Rounds 2–3 are a rough pass (2026-11-04).
    - **Finale:** finishing Round 3 unlocks the big finale mods (bought from Reiko) and the three Myth Driver races, in this order: Touge #1 → Wangan #2 → Street #3; then the Fushigi-Yama summit and the ending.
    - The main plot stays grounded; JRPG weirdness and extra lore go in substories and side quests.

12. **Free crew choice, locked ladder (2026-11-10).** Within a round the PC chooses which crew to challenge. Once the PC starts a crew's ladder they're locked in until they beat that crew's boss, because the other crews won't take on the PC while the PC is already in a feud with another crew. Between rounds, reputation still gates rematches: a beaten crew won't grant a rematch until the PC has proven themselves against the other districts' crews. Lore only, not a tracked stat.

13. **Player character.**
    - Mid-20s, any gender. Moved to the city for work, but is really running from something: the PC was their hometown crew's driver and left the week of a big race for a city job without telling anyone. The crew believes the PC ditched them for something better.
    - **Real reason (locked):** the PC felt like the weak link, believed the crew would do better without them, and left rather than say so. The crew never saw it that way. A misread, like the Myth Drivers', mirroring Sabine, who was pushed out over a lie.
    - **The hook:** in Round 1 the PC hears Sabine's story (walked away from her crew without explanation; some assume she had something to hide) and recognizes their own. Nobody calls Sabine a snitch there; the parallel is implied.
    - Helping Sabine reconcile is the PC working through their own guilt by proxy. After her parting shot at the summit, Sabine turns it back on them: "And when are you calling them?" After the rift (15) the mirror is literal: the PC accused Sabine on bad data, as the Myth Drivers did.
    - **Epilogue (locked):** the PC makes the call themselves: "I was wrong. Be there." Nobody else brings the crew (no clips or luck), or the arc wouldn't resolve. The crew comes to Fushigi-Yama. After a short, awkward apology they don't fully forgive yet, they break the tension with a race ("Talk's cheap. Race us." — first pass): a low-stakes, just-for-fun downhill run that doesn't change the ending either way, possibly with Sabine, Jesse and Reiko watching. Race tuning is Barry's.

14. **Why Sabine takes the PC in.** On the surface: she challenged the PC on the back road, feels personally responsible for the blown motor, and fronts the tow and rebuild on the condition they pay her back. Underneath: she sees herself in the PC, someone who showed up alone and won't say why. She never admits the second. She stepped away from the scene and doesn't run a racing crew or look to build standing with the crews (24).

15. **The PC puts it together — wrongly.**
    - Nobody tells the PC about the snitch accusation. They work it out from clues in the crew reveals across Rounds 1–3, and the clues are bad data that genuinely point toward Sabine's guilt.
    - **Mentor Confrontation (end of Round 3):** the PC accuses Sabine of being the snitch. She doesn't defend herself: she's been accused once already and won't waste the energy again (tired, not proud). After a long silence: "Get out of my garage."
    - **Jesse and Reiko at the rift (first pass 2026-11-05, needs more passes):** Reiko is stung personally — she let the PC in, and the accusation burns her for vouching. Jesse, usually the one who softens things, refuses to smooth it over and quietly points out that the PC decided without asking (subverting the Prologue beat where he covered for Reiko's edge). During the Finale, Reiko goes strictly business (parts/shop stay open mechanically) and Jesse stays quiet/strained (tuning/respec stay open). Both still hang around the garage; the warmth is gone. Because the PC and Reiko warmed up to each other over time (from "Wholesale is for crew. You're not crew." to wholesale to "Sabine's crew"), she's still willing to sell the PC parts after the confrontation: strictly business, but the door stays open. That's why the shop and the big Round 3 mods stay available during the Finale (Austin, 2026-11-10).
    - The rift stands going into the Finale. The PC knows they messed up; the Finale races are how they learn the sweep was benign (16). The PC made the same mistake the Myth Drivers did.

16. **The PC's motive after the rift.** The PC wants the truth, so they race all three Myth Drivers (Touge #1 → Wangan #2 → Street #3) to settle the story. The contradictions between their accounts (17, 18) unravel it. By the end the PC knows the sweep was benign and each Myth Driver's personal reason (2) was hidden behind the snitch story `<TODO: how each reason comes out in the races>`. Sabine still learns the real reasons only at the summit, from the Myth Drivers.

17. **Myth Driver #2's guilt leaks.** In his Finale race, #2 hints that Sabine may have been a scapegoat, out of guilt. One of the first cracks in the PC's certainty (16), and it foreshadows him coming to the summit to apologize.

18. **Myth Driver #1's contradiction (first pass).** In his Finale race (the first of the three), #1 insists Sabine left on her own the night of the sweep. In the next race #2's hints say she was pushed: the first time two accounts can't both be true, which starts the unraveling (16).

19. **The debt.**
    - Sabine fronted the engine rebuild, labor and tow after feeling responsible for the blowout (14), so the PC races to pay off the repair tab until they're even. It's a personal debt, not a crew sponsorship: Sabine stepped away from the scene and isn't trying to make a name for her garage (24).
    - Until it's cleared, a share of each win (not all of it) goes to the debt, so money for mods is tight in Round 1. Share size, debt amount and Reiko's wholesale discount are Barry's `<TBD: Barry>`.
    - **Opening (2026-11-04):** the first three races are more or less scripted. Once mods unlock, the whole world opens up: the PC can take wild races or search out lieutenants (the crew members; "lieutenant" is the sim's encounter tier between wild races and the boss).
    - **Mods (2026-11-04; updated 2026-11-05):** mods unlock after the PC wins race 3. Reiko sees the PC is serious and finally lets them buy parts through her, at full price. Beating story races up each crew's ladder (crew members/lieutenants as well as crew bosses) expands her paid shop stock, with crew bosses gating rare shop mods (28; tier/catalog rules are Barry's). Wholesale follows at the end of Round 1 as she warms up.
    - **Wholesale:** at the end of Round 1 Reiko warms up and gives the PC wholesale prices, even though the debt isn't cleared yet (payoff of "Wholesale is for crew. You're not crew."). By Round 3 the PC has plenty of access to parts.

Clearing it triggers aero mods and Sabine's drinks at the speakeasy (24). After that the PC stays for the fun and to uncover Sabine's mystery.

20. **Not everyone holds it against her.** Most of the scene isn't bitter toward Sabine; they respect her and are puzzled by why she walked away. Suspicion comes from a few voices (gossiping kids, people repeating rumors), not from everyone.

21. **The Wangan crew boss is a promoter.**

22. **The Street crew boss is an influencer.** Young-ish and cocksure, he built his following on the street scene after the old generation broke up. Sabine and the Myth Drivers are relics to him. He reflects modern car culture (clout, builds made for the camera), in sharp contrast to the old generation. First pass: he idolized #3 growing up and felt abandoned when #3 left to "go pro".

    - Her friends accused her instead of showing up for her, and nobody has acknowledged what she went through: her third reason not to rejoin (5).

Separate win gate: winning scripted race 3 is what makes Reiko take the PC seriously and open parts sales (19). How losses are tracked and whether races can be retried is `<TBD: Barry>`.

## Racer Types and Archetypes

"Racer type" is the story role (Delinquent, Salaryman, ...). "Archetype" is the driving style from the sim (`sim/velocity_shift/`). Each district has exactly one racer type per archetype; each racer type always uses its archetype. Only named rivals may break the pattern, on purpose.

| Archetype | Touge | Wangan | Street |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aggressive**: attacks right away | Delinquent | Executive | Punk |
| **Defensive**: waits, then punishes your habits | Gang Leader | Office Lady | Mechanic |
| **Precise**: always makes the smartest move | Drifter | Doctor | Engineer |
| **Balanced**: follows a set rhythm | High Schooler | Salaryman | Skater |
| **Adaptive**: cautious early, sharper later | Honor Student | Consultant | Influencer |

Never reuse archetype names as character or racer-type names. Beating crew members expands Reiko's paid shop stock (28); crew boss stakes are money, reputation (lore only, 12), rare shop mods and story progress; losing a crew boss race means a retry, never losing the car.

## Backstory Timeline

1. **The old generation.** Sabine races alongside the three Myth Drivers. Pressure builds privately: everyone in the group knows her uncle-dad is a detective, and #1 never trusts her because of it; #2 resents the turned-down date. Sabine never told anyone; #2 vented about it to #1 and #3 himself one night, they teased him for weeks, and he convinced himself the jokes started with her; #3 wants out to go pro and dreads telling her he's leaving.
2. **The police sweep.** A large, coordinated sweep hits all three districts (Touge, Wangan, Street) in one night: lots of tickets and impounds, maybe a few arrests, but routine enforcement, not a tip-off. Minutes before it starts, Sabine gets a call from her uncle-dad about an unrelated family emergency (he's had a heart attack) and leaves; she's the only one of the old generation who walks away clean. The scene goes quiet for months afterward (the "collapse"). Privately, the three Myth Drivers accuse Sabine of being the snitch, each using it as cover for his own reason, and freeze her out quietly. The accusation never leaves the four of them.
3. **The breakup.** The old generation fractures. To the wider scene, Sabine simply walked away from the old generation without a word around the time of the sweep. She stays on the scene but keeps clear of every crew. The Myth Drivers stop speaking to each other and fade into semi-retired legend. #3's pro career dies with the sweep (impounded and charged; the team courting him drops him; 9). Sabine opens her own garage off the scene (24), still believing the accusation was the reason.
4. **The player arrives.** The player moves to the city at dusk. A first race with Sabine ends in a blown engine; feeling responsible, she tows the car to her garage, fronts the rebuild, and the player enters the scene owing her the repair tab. Underneath, she sees herself in the PC (unspoken).
5. **Rounds 1–3.** Each round the player beats the same three crews and their crew bosses (running through each whole crew from its kids to its mature members to its old heads to the boss, and each crew leveled up since last time), one crew at a time in whichever order the player chooses within each round, earning the reputation for the next rematch; every crew defeat reveals another piece of the mystery of her disappearance, and in Round 1 the PC recognizes their own story in hers. After each round's crew in his district is cleared, that district's Myth Driver makes a sighting (first sighting in Round 1; talk only, no race; never lays out the sweep). Sabine deflects. The PC pieces together the accusation from misleading clues in the crew reveals and, at the end of Round 3, accuses Sabine of being the snitch. She doesn't defend herself ("Get out of my garage."), and the relationship blows up.
6. **The Myth Drivers.** Finishing Round 3 unlocks a race with each Myth Driver: Touge (#1), Wangan (#2), Street (#3). Knowing they messed up, the player races them while estranged from Sabine to find the truth. Their accounts contradict each other, the snitch story unravels, and the PC learns the sweep was benign and each Myth Driver's real reason.
7. **The blowup.** The PC goes to Sabine: "I know what really happened." She blows up at the PC for digging into her past, and the PC leaves. The PC convinces all three Myth Drivers to come and apologize too: one big apology party.
8. **The summit.** The PC and Sabine race at the summit of Fushigi-Yama `<TODO: how the PC gets Sabine to the final race>`. The PC and the three Myth Drivers apologize around the race `<TODO: before, during or after the race>`. Sabine learns the real reasons for the first time, from them.
9. **Ending.** They reconcile and offer Sabine her place back. After the PC's apology, she calmly declines (her three reasons: decision 5) and gets in one angry parting shot. Then she asks the PC, "And when are you calling them?"
10. **Epilogue.** The PC calls their hometown crew ("I was wrong. Be there."). The crew comes to Fushigi-Yama, the PC apologizes, and they run a low-stakes race for fun. The PC ends the game part of the scene with both crews.
